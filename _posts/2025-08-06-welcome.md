A lot of researchers I admire maintain blogs or participate in a lot of expository mathematical writing. I've been wanting do something like this for years, but I've stopped myself since I didn't think I was at the level where my perspectives would be useful. 

As I've gone through graduate school, however, I've realized that the perspectives I appreciate the most tend to be from near-peers - PhD students and early-career postdocs who learned about whatever it is they are writing about more recently and still remember how they came to understand something, whether it's a fundamental derivation or a novel research idea. 

To that end, I've decided to try my hand at writing a math blog! There are a lot of proofs and papers on my to-read list right now, and I want to get to the point where I really understand them and can hopefully offer my own perspective on their usefulness, instead of just being able to state what they do. I've been told that the best way to understand something is to teach it or communicate it, which is something I used to think a lot about in college when I was studying for my humanities coursework. Strangely enough, I never applied this thinking to my math studies - until now!

I'm going to try to make this accessible to a sophomore or junior undergraduate student in mathematics (or at least the kind of sophomore or junior undergraduate student I was in mathematics, at a small liberal arts college with no computer science department and little access to cutting-edge computational science research). 

I’m also just generally a big believer that there is no such thing as being too detailed when describing mathematical proofs or derivations. Of course, one should be explicit about which components of a derivation are simply rungs on a ladder to the next big insight and which are actually novel or unique to the proof. However, I think all details have the potential to be meaningful to a reader. I hope to hone my writing skills to be better at this kind of writing through this blog.

If you have any recommendations on things I should read about, critiques or criticisms (please do criticize! I want to get better!) about my writing or any derivations I present, or any other thoughts, please do feel free to reach out! Hopefully this is also a good way to make some new mathematical friends!

Thank you for reading this!
